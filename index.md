## The Core Insight

AI-assisted development isn't about replacing syntax with automation.

It's about **prompting systems** to reveal their patterns, then **engineering solutions** with that understanding.

As Pierre Bourdieu showed, **linguistic capital** - fluency in the languages that matter - converts to social and economic mobility. In a digitized society, those languages include Python, APIs, prompts, and systems thinking.

**LLMs democratize access to these languages.** But **the syntax barrier is not the systems barrier**. Production engineering still demands models of reality: data contracts, state, auth, testing, deploys, cost, and risk.

That's what I explore here.

## Why This Matters

**AI tools are changing accessibility to engineering.**

When I was a kid, I loved The Sims, RollerCoaster Tycoon, and K'Nex. I wanted to build and engineer things, but I didn't get exposed to engineering growing up.

The syntax of programming languages was a gatekeeper. Unless you learned that specialized language, you couldn't touch the parts.

**LLMs crack that gate.** They give access to language - not just English, but Python, SQL, API schemas, and prompt dialects.

But here's the catch: **LLMs collapse the syntax barrier, not the systems barrier.**

You can "make it run" without knowing why it runs. New gatekeepers emerge: infrastructure, model access, data ownership, cloud costs, and governance.

That's the shift I'm documenting. From **syntax to systems**. From **translation tax to infrastructure access**.
